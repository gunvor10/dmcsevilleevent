Europe’s Oldest City

Founded by the Phoenicians over 3,000 years ago, Cádiz claims to be the oldest continuously inhabited city in Western Europe, its old town packed onto a narrow peninsula surrounded by the Atlantic.

Sherry Country

Cádiz sits at the heart of the Sherry Triangle alongside Jerez de la Frontera and Sanlúcar de Barrameda, giving groups easy access to traditional bodega visits and tastings.

A Bay Built for Sailing

The wide, sheltered bay of Cádiz offers ideal conditions for sailing regattas and watersport team building, distinct from the closed harbours of other coastal cities.

Carnival Spirit

Home to Spain’s most famous Carnival celebrations, Cádiz carries a playful, informal energy that suits lighter, social incentive programs.

Uncrowded Atlantic Beaches

Unlike the busier Costa del Sol, Cádiz’s beaches remain relatively undiscovered by international tourism, offering space and quiet for group downtime.

A Distinctive Alternative

Close enough to Seville for easy transfers, Cádiz offers a coastal, historic character that stands apart from Andalusia’s larger inland cities.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions — Cádiz

What is the nearest airport to Cádiz?
Jerez Airport (XRY) is the closest, roughly 35 km from Cádiz. Málaga Airport is an additional option, approximately 2h40 by bus.
What makes Cádiz different from other Andalusian destinations?
Cádiz combines sherry culture, one of the oldest historic centres in Europe and a bay well suited to sailing activities, offering a distinctive alternative to Seville.
Can DMC Seville Event combine Cádiz with other destinations?
Yes — Cádiz is often combined with Seville or Jerez sherry bodegas as part of a multi-destination Andalusian program.
What is the best time of year to visit Cádiz?
Late spring through early autumn offers warm weather for beach and sailing activities, with February’s Carnival a lively alternative.
How many people can a Cádiz program accommodate?
Beachfront and old-town venues comfortably handle groups from 20 to 150 participants.
