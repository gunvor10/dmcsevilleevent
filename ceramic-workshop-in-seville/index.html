TRIANA

The History of Triana and Its Ceramics Traditions

If you cross the Guadalquivir River into the neighborhood of Triana, you’re entering the historic center of Seville’s ceramic craft. For centuries, this was where artisans shaped and painted tiles and pottery that would go on to decorate churches, convents, fountains, and homes all over Spain and Latin America.

Triana’s proximity to the river made it perfect for pottery — with rich clay, water for mixing and glazing, and access to trade routes. Even today, walking through its streets, you’ll find studios and old kilns that have been firing tiles since the 16th century.

Many of the workshops we organize take place in this iconic district, either in modern artisan studios or historic ceramic spaces that have preserved the charm of old Seville.

A Living Craft With a Story

Ceramics in Seville aren’t just art — they’re a legacy. The tradition dates back to Roman and Islamic times, and each tile tells a part of that history. When you take part in a ceramic workshop, you’re not just making a souvenir — you’re continuing a story that began hundreds of years ago, right there in Triana.

FAQ

Ceramic workshops: common questions

Do guests need any artistic experience?+

No. The workshops are designed for beginners, and local artisans guide every step, from the first sketch to the final glaze.

Where do the ceramic workshops take place?+

Most workshops take place in Triana, Seville’s historic ceramics district, in artisan studios or historic ceramic spaces. We can also bring a tile-painting workshop to your hotel or event venue.

Can guests take their piece home the same day?+

Painted tiles need to be glazed and fired in a kiln. Depending on your schedule, pieces are delivered to your hotel before departure or sent on afterwards.

How many people can take part?+

Tile painting works for groups from around 8 up to 80 guests. Pottery on the wheel suits smaller groups, as each guest needs their own wheel.

Can the workshop be combined with food and wine?+

Yes. Many groups pair the workshop with a tapas lunch in Triana, or turn it into a crafts-themed afternoon with food, wine, and music.
