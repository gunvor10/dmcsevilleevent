A Sea of Olive Trees

Jaén province produces more olive oil than any other region on earth, its rolling landscape covered by an estimated 66 million olive trees stretching to the horizon.

Renaissance Splendour

The city’s old town is considered one of the finest examples of Spanish Renaissance architecture, anchored by a monumental 16th-century Cathedral.

Santa Catalina Castle

A Moorish hilltop castle overlooking the city, Santa Catalina offers panoramic views over the olive groves and now houses a parador, ideal for exclusive evening events.

Liquid Gold

Olive oil tastings, mill visits and pairing dinners give corporate groups a hands-on, sensory activity unique to this region.

Authentic & Uncrowded

Far from Andalusia’s main tourist routes, Jaén offers a genuine, unhurried character well suited to smaller, more exclusive incentive groups.

Sierra de Cazorla

Spain’s largest protected natural park lies within reach, offering hiking and nature excursions for groups wanting an active add-on.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions — Jaén

What is the nearest airport to Jaén?
Málaga Airport is the best option, approximately 2 hours 30 minutes by bus.
What is Jaén known for?
Jaén sits at the centre of the world’s largest olive grove landscape and is known for its Renaissance architecture and hilltop castle.
Is Jaén a good fit for smaller, exclusive incentive groups?
Yes — its authentic, less-touristic character makes Jaén well suited to smaller groups seeking a more exclusive Andalusian experience.
What is the best time of year to visit Jaén?
Spring, when the olive groves are green and temperatures are mild, and autumn during the olive harvest are both excellent times to visit.
How many people can a Jaén program accommodate?
Given its more intimate character, Jaén typically suits groups from 15 to 80 participants.
Can Jaén be combined with other destinations?
Yes — Jaén is often combined with Granada or Córdoba as part of a broader Andalusian itinerary.
